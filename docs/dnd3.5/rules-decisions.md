# D&D 3.5: rules decisions

How the D&D 3.5 ruleset reads its rules where the SRD is ambiguous, and where it deliberately differs from them. Each ruling is settled: change it only on purpose, and update this page with it. A ruling the engine holds names the code that holds it.

## Characters

- **An Intelligence increase grants skill points retroactively.** Every level counts its skill points with the character's current skill-point ability modifier (`SkillRules.levelPoints`, read by the skills component), so raising Intelligence adds points to the levels already taken. The PHB's non-retroactive reading was weighed and set aside.
- **Skill points count the permanent Intelligence.** The skill-point ability's modifier a level counts is its permanent score's (`AbilitiesComponent.getPermanentModifier`, read by `SkillsComponent.getLevelPointsPerLevel`): its base, its levels' increases and what its race's modifiers make of its misc, applied as the sheet applies them (a half-orc's −2, `Builds.prepareSheet`); an item's enhancement or a spell's bonus aside, which the sheet's Intelligence counts. The language picks read the same score. A character whose levels spent more points than that leaves (a half-orc saved before the rule) keeps its ranks: its sheet flags the overspent points, and a level-up's or a level edit's save is refused with them unless forced, as any issue the character has, until an edit brings the ranks within the budget.
- **A class's ability boosts are misc bonuses.** A class that raises an ability (the Dragon Disciple's) targets `abilities.<name>.misc`, like every class, feat and item bonus; `abilities.<name>.level` isn't a modifier path. The SRD's "as if gained from character level advancement" (which would count the boost toward skill points) was set aside.
- **No epic levels or spells.** A character stops at level 20 whatever its classes (`MAX_CHARACTER_LEVEL`), a class at level 20 (`MAX_CLASS_LEVEL`, `vocabulary/dnd3.5/classes.ts`), spell levels run 0 to 9 (`MAX_SPELL_LEVEL`), and bonus caster levels never take a class past 20 (`BonusCasterLevels`). Epic material in a source is left out.
- **A caster level is a class's own, and the highest counts.** A class's caster level is its level and the caster levels other classes add to it (`classes.<name>.bonuscasterlevel`, a prestige class's "+1 level of existing spellcasting class"), up to 20: the class level its spells per day are read at (`SpellcastingState.casterLevelOf`). A class counts once it has slots at a spell level, cantrips included. A multiclass character's caster levels don't add up: a prerequisite's "Caster level 5th" checks the highest (`spellcasting.casterlevel`), "Arcane caster level 5th" an arcane class's highest (`spellcasting.arcanecasterlevel`; `SpellcastingState.highestCasterLevel`). A paladin's or a ranger's caster level is his class level from his fourth level, not half of it as the PHB has it: no class's data says it halves its caster level.
- **A shield's maximum Dexterity is its slot's,** as an armor's is: the AC caps the Dexterity bonus by both, and a modifier reaches it (`ShieldsComponent`).
- **AC is per weapon set; skills and speed take the worst case (a shield carried in any set).** A character has no single armor class: each weapon set has its own, AC, touch and flat-footed, which counts a shield only in the set that holds it, its enhancement too, and the parts every set shares (armor, natural armor, deflection, dodge, misc; `ArmorClass.defenseOf`). A modifier on the shield bonus applies in each set, its own shield's (`Builds.scopesOf`). A gate on holding a shield, or on the armor class, in a modifier on the armor class follows the set, met by a set that meets it whole, so a monk's AC bonus counts in each set without a shield (`Builds.scopesOf`, `CombatPaths.resolve`), and a set's attacks take only its own shield's penalties: its check penalty without proficiency or on a finessed attack, a tower shield's −2 (`Attacks.gearPenalty`, `shieldCheckPenalty`). The skills and the speed have one value each: a shield carried in any set weighs on them, the skills taking the worst set's check penalty (`ShieldsComponent.getCheckPenalty`), and a gate elsewhere reads whether any set holds one (`combat.shield.held`). A character with no weapon set has one loadout, with no shield. An item held in several sets is one inventory entry (its sets, its `groups`): each set holds it, for its AC and its attacks, and it weighs once, its check penalty weighing on the skills once (`ItemPlacement.heldSets`, read by the inventory, combat and shields components); each set it's in must have room for it, refused naming the first that hasn't (`Equipping.describeConflict`).
- **A character's languages are those its modifiers grant it, and its picks of any language but a secret one, which we read past the SRD on purpose.** It speaks what a modifier grants it, `languages.<name>.known` set true (`LanguagesComponent`, the grant given as its modifier applies in the build's rounds, behind its requirements as any modifier, as a feat's possession is: `PossessesVirtually`): its race's automatic languages (a dwarf's Common and Dwarven), a class level's (the druid's Druidic, at its 1st level, while his alignment meets that level's requirements, which gate that level's own modifiers alone, Druidic and the level's spell slots: a Lawful Good druid 1 keeps the class features the level grants, Wild Empathy, Nature Sense, his animal companion and his proficiencies), a feat's or an item's. A grant is never stored and never removed: the sheet shows it fixed. Past those, it picks, and its picks are stored (`SpokenLanguages`, by `LanguageRules`): any language but a secret one, one a point of the modifier of its permanent Intelligence (`AbilitiesComponent.getPermanentModifier`, which its skill points count too: its current score, so a raised Intelligence adds picks retroactively as it adds skill points, with what its race's modifiers make of the score's misc, applied as the sheet applies them, a half-orc's −2 or an `abilities.*.misc` one, an item's enhancement or a spell's bonus aside), one per whole Speak Language rank (a cross-class half rank adds none), and one per extra pick its modifiers add (`languages.extrapicks`: a loremaster's 4th and 8th levels, a mage of the Arcane Order's 3rd and 6th). There's no bonus language: the SRD's lists a race and a class pick from (a dwarf's Giant, Gnome…, a cleric's Celestial, Abyssal and Infernal, the wizard's "may substitute Draconic") are deliberately left out, the parser reading none of them, so a dwarf picks Sylvan as freely as Giant. A secret language (its type `Secret`, however it's cased, `LanguageRules.isSecret`: Druidic) is a character's only by a grant: no pick takes one, as the druid's text forbids teaching Druidic to nondruids. A requirement on `languages.<name>.known` reads a language however the character speaks it, picked or granted. A languages edit refuses, naming it, a new pick that's secret or that the room its kept picks leave doesn't take (`SpokenLanguages.plan`, through the characters part's `planLinkPicks` of the `speaks` link); a pick the character has is never refused, so an edit that only removes picks always saves, and a character over its room (one saved before the rule, or whose Intelligence dropped) keeps its picks. The languages picker offers only what one more pick fits (`describeLinkPicks`).
- **Encumbered speed is one rule:** two thirds of the speed, rounded up to the next 5 ft. (`EncumbranceComponent.getEncumberedSpeed`). It gives the SRD table's speeds exactly, and every other speed the same way (25 ft. → 20 ft.).

## Creating a character

- **The ability-score methods are the dialog's guide.** Point buy's budget, the standard array and the rolls set the scores in the form; the server checks each score against its bounds only (`describeCreation().scores`), since the request doesn't say which method made them.

## Levelling up

- **A 1st level's hit points are rolled (or entered) like any level's,** within 1 and the hit die. The SRD's maximum at 1st level isn't enforced: tables differ.
- **A spell is known once per class list:** a level can't pick a spell its pool knows already, picked at another level (saved, or earlier in the same plan), granted by a class level (a later one's, or a later planned one's, too), or made known by a modifier there; another class's list can know it again (a sorcerer's Light, then a wizard's). The save and the edit refuse such a pick, forced or not, naming the spell and the list (`SelectionChecks.checkPicksNotHeld`, through `LevelsPlanning`); the preview drops it, as the save would refuse it (`withoutHeldPicks`), and the picker leaves it out, read off the character with every level it has and plans (`LevelPicker.holder`). A repeat saved before the rule stays: an edit refuses only a new pick, never the level's own saved ones. What a list knows without a pick goes by the list too: a list that knows every spell of a level knows those another class's list has (a cleric/wizard's cleric list, the cleric spells of his spellbook, at the cleric's DC and with his domains' tags: `KnownSpells`), and a spell a modifier makes known on a list stands beside the same spell another list knows, once on its list however many modifiers give it (`PossessesVirtually`). A spell several lists know applies its own modifiers once (`DetailedCharacterDataLoader.sourcesOf`).
- **A feat that doesn't stack is had once:** a level can't pick one the character has already, picked at another level (saved, a later one too, or earlier in the same plan), granted by a class level (a later one's, or a later planned one's, too), or made possessed by a modifier (an elf's Martial Weapon Proficiency: Longsword, an item's, the character's own). The save and the edit refuse such a pick, forced or not, naming the feat (`SelectionChecks.checkPicksNotHeld`, through `LevelsPlanning`, over the feats the ruleset's character holds without the picks, `getHeldFeats`); the preview and the edit's steps drop it, as the save would refuse it (`withoutHeldPicks`), and the picker leaves it out, read off the character with every level it has and plans (`LevelPicker.holder`). A pick saved before the rule stays: an edit refuses only a new pick, never the level's own saved ones. A feat that stacks is the level's to pick however the character has it.
- **A pick given by another of the level's picks gives way:** when one of a level's (or a plan's) picks gives what another takes, through its modifiers (`set feats.<slug>.possessed`, `set spells.<spell>.<list>.known`), the given pick gives way and the giving one stays, as when another level holds it: a cleric's Weapon Focus: Longsword as a General feat beside his War Domain Weapon: Longsword, which gives it; a spell another spell picked at the level makes known on its list (homebrew: no seeded spell does it). It holds for a feat that doesn't stack and for a spell on its list, whichever kind of pick gives it. The save and the edit refuse the given pick, forced or not, with the messages above; the preview and the edit's steps drop it, its slot free for another pick; the pickers leave it out, read off the character with the feats and spells picked so far (`LevelPicker.holder`). One rule holds the three (`SelectionChecks.checkPicksNotHeld` and `withoutHeldPicks`, through a save's and an edit's `LevelsPlanning`, and the preview's and an edit's steps' `LevelUpState.fitPicks`): a pick the character's modifiers give (`given`, which `PossessesVirtually` marks on `getHeldFeats` and `getHeldSpells` as a grant applies) gives way when the character without it holds it all the same. The outcome doesn't depend on the order the picks were made in; of two picks that each give the other, the one whose name comes first alphabetically stays and the other gives way. A level saved before the rule with such a pair stays: an edit refuses only a new pick.
- **An overfull pool is refused, never trimmed.** A level-up's save or edit that picks more feats or spells than a pool has room for is refused with the pool and its room, forced or not (`PicksDistribution.refuseOverfull`, through `LevelsPlanning`). No pick is dropped silently; the wizard's steps answer each pool's room so it never sends too many.
- **A class's levels are taken in order, forced or not.** A level-up's class level is its class's next: one past the character's levels in the class, saved and planned before it (`LevelRules.findNextClassLevel`, through `LevelsPlanning`), as a PCGen import takes them. A force waives a class level's requirements, never its place: Fighter 3 as a first level is refused, "Fighter 3 can't be taken there: Fighter 1 comes next". So a character's level in a class, which its levels in it count (`classes.<class>.level`, the sheet's header, its card's `ClassLevels.countByClass`), is its highest class level of it too. A character a forced level left with a gap before the rule (a lone Fighter 3) takes the levels before it in turn (Fighter 2); once its next is one it holds (Fighter 3 again), no level of the class comes next: the class picker leaves the class out, and the save refuses any of its levels.
- **A stackable feat can be taken twice at one level,** in one pool or two: a human fighter 1 can take Toughness in both its General slots. Each pick is a row of its own (a `feats` pick, `character.advancement_picks`), the preview and the wizard keep both, and a plan's pick lands on whichever level its pool's slot is at, the same level or another. A feat that doesn't stack is refused when a level picks it twice or the character has it already (above), and a spell when a level picks it twice (`SelectionChecks`); the preview keeps a pool's repeat of either once, and the pickers leave out what any of the character's levels holds, saved or planned, a later one too (`LevelPicker.holder`).
- **The skills step's "Auto" is a convenience, not a rule.** It spreads the points at random within the caps the engine answers, class and cross-class skills alike; a player adjusts what it picks.
- **A ruleset's progression is its Settings', and reaches its characters as they are.** The feats each character level gives and the levels that raise an ability are the ruleset's Settings (Feat Advancement, Ability Score Increases: `RULESET_FIELDS`, read by `LevelRules.of`), each a frequency and more at the levels it names, the core rules' unless its chain saves its own. A frequency runs past level 20, so epic levels, were the app to have them, would follow it. An increase saved at a level the Settings no longer give one counts for nothing (`DetailedCharacterDataLoader.readAbilityPicks`), its level's edit opens without it and writes it away, and one the Settings newly give is asked for on its level's edit; a lowered feat count leaves the general pool overfull until an edit fits it. A bonded creature's feats at its hit dice stay the Monster Manual's, one and one per three hit dice, whatever the Settings (`CREATURE_FEAT_HIT_DICE_INTERVAL`).

## Importing a character

How a character another program saved (PCGen, `engine/rulesets/dnd3.5/imports/`) becomes a 3.5 character. The import ends with a report of what it imported and every line of the file it couldn't match: nothing is dropped silently.

- **The file says what it is, before a ruleset is picked.** The program that saved it (PCGen's opens on `PCGVERSION:` and names its `VERSION:`), that program's version, the file's format and the rules its character is of (PCGen's game mode, `GAMEMODE:35e`) are read from it (`Engine.imports().describeFile`, each base rules' importers asked): the rulesets of the base rules that import it alone are offered. The 3.5 rules import PCGen's 35e characters (`Dnd35Imports`); a PCGen file of another game mode (Pathfinder) is refused with why, its rules not 3.5's, and so is a format no reader reads yet. The format picks the reader (PCGen's 2.0, `PcgFormat2`, which PCGen 5 and 6 both write). Names are matched in the picked ruleset, so a custom ruleset's own classes and items are found (`PcgenNames`).
- **The character is leveled up one level at a time.** Each level is a level-up of its own, checked as one (`LevelByLevel`, through `LevelsPlanning`), so it builds and validates like a character made in the app. A level the checks find fault with (a pool the file left unspent, points it spent past what the app gives) is saved anyway, as a forced level-up is, and the report says what the checks found. The levels stop at the first one the ruleset can't take (a class it hasn't, a class's level out of its order, one a level-up refuses even forced), which the report lists with those after it; the ability increases are those of the levels taken.
- **Feats and skill ranks are placed on a level.** PCGen lists the feats and each skill's total ranks, not the level each was taken at. A feat goes on the first level with room in a pool it can be picked in whose requirements the character meets there, the pool the file picked it in first (`PicksPlacement`): Weapon Specialization never before Fighter 4. A skill's ranks go on the levels of the class they were bought in, within each level's points and maximum ranks, the ranks that can't wait for a later level first. A cleric's domain is its domain's feat, in the Cleric Domain pool. What no level takes is listed.
- **The file's scores are kept: its record of an increase is followed where the ruleset gives one.** A level takes the ability increases the ruleset's Settings give it (`LevelRules`), each from the changes PCGen recorded at that level first, else the highest score's; the starting scores are the file's less the increases placed, so the character ends on the file's scores. A change PCGen recorded where the ruleset gives no increase (a manual edit at 9th level, a −1 at 11th) is kept in the starting score, and the report says so. A starting score that would fall outside a new character's bounds (`describeCreation`: 1 to 100) is brought to them, which the report says too.
- **Magic gear is listed for the user to make, never made.** A piece of PCGen's custom magic gear (a base item and its enhancements: "Full Plate" at +3) that the ruleset has no item of by its name is listed with its base item: the import makes nothing in a ruleset, which may be one the user can't edit. A plain custom item (an outfit PCGen renamed) is found by its base item's name.
- **A subrace the ruleset hasn't comes in as its race.** PCGen's "Elf (Drow)" is found as the ruleset's "Drow Elf" or one of its other wordings, else as the race it's a kind of, Elf, which the report says: its racial traits are the base race's.
- **Gear comes in as the app holds it.** A plain custom item the ruleset has only the base item of (a masterwork longsword) comes in as that item, and the report says what sets it apart is lost. What PCGen's equipment set doesn't carry (`Not Carried`, what a container there holds, or what's left out of the set) comes in carried, as the app's inventory is what a character carries, and the report says so. A wand keeps the charges the file says are left; a pair of one weapon (`Two Weapons`) takes a hand each; gear worn where the app has no place for it is carried, which the report says.
- **The file's text is kept where the app keeps it.** Its biography goes to the description, its other text (eyes, hair, money…) to the notes, each labelled, and its DM notes to the private notes, which the sheet keeps from its readers.
- **Spells and feats are imported as the character's choices, never made.** The import picks the ruleset's own spells and feats by name, as a level-up picks them; it never makes a spell or a feat in a ruleset (nor an item: magic gear is listed). A spell the character chose to know (PCGen's book of them, `Known Spells`: a sorcerer's, a bard's, a wizard's spellbook) goes on the first level whose class's spell list has room for it at its spell level, its list the one of the class PCGen knows it as (`PicksPlacement`); one the ruleset hasn't, or that list hasn't, is listed, and so is one no level has room for (a wizard's spellbook past the two spells a level learns). Left out, none a choice: a spell its class knows all of (a cleric's, which a file saved without PCGen's auto-known spells holds), one something else makes known (PCGen's `SOURCE` a domain's, a feat's, an item's), and a day's preparation (PCGen's other books). Two spells of one name (a list's own and an inherited one) are told apart by the class's list, and two lists of a class by the spell level PCGen gives.
- **Left out: what the rules work out, and what isn't imported yet.** Class features, weapon proficiencies and PCGen's own entries (its settings, its pools, the abilities its rules give) come from the class and race. A class feature's choice (a favored enemy, a combat style), a template the character took (a half-celestial's; PCGen's own, which it applies to every character of a race or a class, are left out) and a companion are listed, with why. A value past what the app keeps (a name past 255 characters, experience past a whole number column's largest) is brought within it, and one it has no word for (a gender) takes its default, which the report says.

## Content

- **A school's Spell Focus stays.** The Spell Focus and Greater Spell Focus feats a school generates aren't removed with a spell (`SpellFocusFeats`), unlike a skill's Skill Focus, which goes with its skill (`SkillFocusFeats`).
- **An item is made from a template of its own type.** Only a weapon, an armor or a shield is a template (`TEMPLATE_ITEM_TYPES`): an item's save refuses a template of another type, and a template's edit refuses a type change while items of another type are made from it, in its ruleset's view or a ruleset built on it, whose views read it, not in another fork, whose view reads the original or its own copy (`ItemEntity`).
- **A class level may give 0 skill points.** A familiar uses its master's skills, so its seeded levels hold 0, and the class level form accepts it.
- **A geomancer's drift is of his level's stage.** Each geomancer level picks a drift of the stage the class table gives it, from Drift 1 at 1st and 2nd level to Drift 5 at 9th and 10th, each stage a pool of its ten drifts (Complete Divine's `geomancer.json` overrides): the class's "appropriate stage". The general drift rules' choice of a lower stage instead isn't offered, so each stage's pick always follows two drifts of the stage before, as those rules ask.
