# Access & Authorization

This is the source of truth for who can do what across the app. Every gated mutation goes through one of the policy classes under `server/services/policies/` — services call them, policies throw `ForbiddenError` / `UnprocessableEntityError` / `ConflictError` (and `NotFoundError` where a refusal mustn't reveal the row: `RulesetsPolicy.canRead`, `CampaignsPolicy.canRead`, `CampaignsPolicy.canSeeCharacter`, `CharactersPolicy.canEdit`, `canEditPortrait`, `canEditRows`, `canReadRows`, `canReadContributors`, `canExport` and its owner's checks (`canArchive`, `canShare`…), and the reads that ask its `isReadable`, through `findReadableCharacter`), the throw propagates. A refusal says why by its status, one way in every policy: a refusal because the record the request acts on is in the wrong state for it (archived or not, a draft or published, a contributor's status: an archived ruleset's edit, a live record's unarchive or permanent delete, an unpublished ruleset's fork) is a `ConflictError` (409), as a write that lost a race is; one because the request names another record that can't be used for it (an extension that isn't a public published one of the base, a ruleset that isn't playable to create on), or asks it of a ruleset whose kind can't take it, whatever its state (forking one that isn't a base, publishing as an extension one that isn't a fork or that subscribes to extensions, an extension subscribing to another, or one in use as an extension, starring a fork for play), is an `UnprocessableEntityError` (422), checked before its state; one because of who's asking is a `ForbiddenError` (403), or the `NotFoundError` that hides the record, checked before either. Another record a request names is checked between the two, by the acted-on record's policy, which takes its facts or its own policy: an unknown one's 404, then its refusals (an extension a fork subscribes to, `canSubscribeExtension`; one it unsubscribes from, `canUnsubscribeExtension`; a character linked to a campaign, `canLinkCharacter`, which refuses an archived one, or one linked to another campaign, as a 422), the acted-on record's own 409 among them (an extension it subscribes to already, a character linked to it already). An archived character's share link and PDF are the exception: they answer the 404 an unknown character gets (`canShare`, `canExport`), as an archived character is out of a link's reach.

**A demo's session** (`Session`'s `isDemo`, whose user expires) is refused by its policies, whatever its standing on the record, what pulls other users in or reaches outside its account (`BasePolicy.canUseSignedUpFeatures`, a `ForbiddenError`: "Sign up to use this feature"): a character's and a ruleset's contributors (`canManageContributors`: inviting, revoking, changing a role), a campaign's players (`canManagePlayers`), a character's share link (`canShare`), its PDF (`canExport`, `CampaignsPolicy.canExportCharacter`; its routes answer every refused export the 404 an unknown character gets, a demo's too), its portrait (`canEditPortrait`), and a ruleset's publishing (`canPublish`). Its `permissions` answer each, so a demo's pages offer none of them; it reads what any reader reads, its characters' contributors too. The account's own features, which no record's policy is asked, are refused by `denyDemoUser` on their routes (its profile, its API keys, its linked accounts, and starting a campaign, whose players would outlive the demo) and its avatar by its policy (`UsersPolicy.canEditAvatar`, the 403 anyone else's gets), and the client hides them on `isDemo`.

**An archived record** is refused in one wording for its kind, its edit, its archive, a contributor's invite or role change, a subscribe or an unsubscribe, an invite's accept, and a character's portrait and its link to a campaign (another record there, a 422) alike: "This campaign is archived" (`CampaignsPolicy.canModify`), "This ruleset is archived" (`RulesetRoles.checkUnarchived`), "This character is archived" (`CharactersPolicy`'s `ARCHIVED_CHARACTER`), which an invite's page shows as its refusal.

If you're adding a new mutation, find the matching row below and call the same policy method. If your case isn't covered, extend the policy — don't write inline `userId === session.userId` checks (see "Identity vs. policy" at the bottom).

A service builds a policy with `await XPolicy.for(db, session, entity)`, through its transaction's handle (a policy whose list asks it of each row builds the page's with `forMany(db, session, entities)`, each what `for` builds: `CharactersPolicy.forMany`, for the campaign roster, and a page of notifications' invites' records, `CampaignsPolicy.forMany` and `RulesetsPolicy.forMany`, whose `for` builds one through them): `for` loads the session's standing on the entity (its contributor role on a ruleset or a character, its player row in a campaign). Every check is then sync: it reads that standing, and what the service passes it about other rows (`canDeleteEntity({ inUse })`, `canHardDelete({ inActiveCampaign })`), and throws or answers. A policy has no other static (`for`, and `forMany` where a list needs one) and no check queries (`arkyvree/policy-shape`); a test may build one with `new`, from a standing it sets.

## Actors

The system has four actor categories. Most policies are an OR of two or three of these.

| Actor | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Owner** | `entity.userId === session.userId`. The user who created the row. Owners always retain rights that contributors don't (publish, archive, manage Admin contributors). |
| **Contributor (Admin / Editor / Viewer)** | An `Active`, non-deleted row in `contributorsInRules` (for rulesets) or `contributorsInCharacter` (for characters), keyed by `(entity.id, session.userId)`. Roles enum: `'Admin' | 'Editor' | 'Viewer'`. |
| **Campaign member (Player / Game Master)** | An `Active`, non-deleted row in `playersInCampaign`, keyed by `(campaign.id, session.userId)`. Roles: `'Player' | 'Game Master'`. |
| **Public visitor** | Anyone with a session. Public rulesets and characters with `visibility = "Public"` are readable by all visitors. |

A request to the public API (`/v1`) acts as the user its API key belongs to: the services read its `Session`, `{ userId }`, as they read a signed-in session's, so every check below holds for it alike (see [public-api.md](./public-api.md)).

A user can hold multiple roles for the same entity (e.g. owner of a ruleset they're also a contributor on — happens via test seed data). Policies check the strongest applicable role.

## Rulesets — `RulesetsPolicy`

`RulesetsPolicy.for(tx, session, ruleset)` loads, as `forMany` does a page's in a query each: the session's contributor role (`contributorsInRules`: a system ruleset has none); on a ruleset that isn't public, whether it has a live seat in a campaign on it, and whether one is in a live campaign (`Players.findSeatPerRuleset(db, { userId, rulesetIds })`: a seat in a campaign archived or not, who may read the campaign, `CampaignsPolicy.canRead`, lets it read the ruleset; one in a live campaign lets it create there); and, on a private ruleset it has no other standing on, whether it edits a character built on it (`Characters.findRulesetIds(db, { editorId, rulesetIds })`: owns one, or actively contributes to one, archived or not; what lets it read it). A removed player's seat is deleted, and a seat is archived only with its user's account, which no session signs in to, so neither gives anything.

| Method | Allowed actors | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `canUpdate({ kind, private })` | owner, **Admin** | edits ruleset metadata (name, description, privacy, archive). Throws on a ruleset without an owner (a system one, or an orphan: see below); then on a kind the edit names that it can't take (`kind`, and what the edit read: `canTakeKind`, a 422); then on Archived rulesets, and on making a public ruleset private (`private`, what the edit asks): who, then its kind, then its state |
| `canUpdateEntity` | owner, **Admin**, **Editor** | edits entity content (feats, items, classes, …). Same archive guard |
| `canDeleteEntity({ inUse, refusal })` | owner, **Admin**, **Editor** | deletes individual entities, and the ones a save's plan removes (a generated feat: `EntityWriter.removeEntity`, in its plan's words, `refusal`). Throws if `inUse` (`EntityUses.refusesDelete`: would orphan a character pick on this ruleset or any descendant fork, or a link whose role refuses it). See [rulesets.md](./rulesets.md#what-inuse-means-in-entity-delete-services) |
| `canPublish(kind)` | **owner only** | Draft → Published; can't be delegated to Admin. As the kind it's published as (`kind`, and what the publish read: `canTakeKind`, a 422), checked before its state (a 409); its `permissions` ask it of its own kind, whether it may be published as it is |
| `canFork` | anyone (any session) | source must be Published and be a base ruleset (`rulesetId IS NULL`) |
| `canTakeKind({ hasExtensions, kind, usedAsExtension })` | anyone (any session) | the kind a publish or an edit leaves the ruleset in, its own too, by what the write read under the ruleset's lock, which a subscribe waits on (`Publishes.readKindFacts`): `canChangeKind` when it changes, and `canBeExtension` for an extension. Publishing and editing ask it inside `canPublish` and `canUpdate`, after who and before the ruleset's state; its `permissions` answer it for each kind (`kinds`), which its publish and edit dialogs offer as they are |
| `canBeExtension` | anyone (any session) | whether the ruleset can be published as an extension, or made one: a fork that subscribes to no extension (`canTakeKind` asks it) |
| `canChangeKind({ usedAsExtension })` | anyone (any session) | whether the ruleset's kind can change: not an extension another ruleset subscribes to (`usedAsExtension`), which would go on depending on what's no extension (`canTakeKind` asks it) |
| `canBeSubscribed({ extensionIds, id, parentId })` | anyone (any session) | the extension a fork subscribes to: never the subscriber itself (`id`), whatever kind either read of it says, published as an extension, public (a system one always is), and a fork of the subscriber's base (`parentId`); one it doesn't subscribe to yet (`extensionIds`, a `ConflictError`). `canSubscribeExtension` asks it of each extension it names, after who and the fork's kind, before the fork's state |
| `canStar` | anyone (any session) | a public base or extension (`isStarrable`, which a list's rows answer too): a fork for play is no bookmark |
| `canAcceptInvite` | anyone (any session) | accepting a contributor's invite (`{ status }`), a pending one (`canAnswerInvite`), on a ruleset that isn't archived (`ConflictError`) |
| `canSubscribeExtension({ extensions, usedAsExtension })` | **owner only** | the host must be a fork that isn't an extension, and no other ruleset may subscribe to it (`usedAsExtension`), each a 422; then the extensions it names (`extensions`: each one's policy, none for one the session can't read, a 404, then each one's `canBeSubscribed`), other records; then live (an archived one, a 409): who, then its kind and the other records, then its state |
| `canUnsubscribeExtension({ inUse, isSubscribed })` | **owner only** | who and the kind `canSubscribeExtension` asks, used as an extension or not; then one it subscribes to (`isSubscribed`, the extension it names: a 404); then its state, as `canSubscribeExtension`'s, and the in-use guard |
| `canManageContributors` | owner, **Admin** | managing contributors, archived or not: what every check below starts with |
| `canManageAdminContributors` | **owner only** | narrowing of the above for touching `Admin`-tier rows |
| `canInviteContributors({ invitee, role })` | owner, **Admin** | an Admin (`role`), the owner only; a user other than its owner (`invitee`, the address's account: `UnprocessableEntityError`); on a ruleset that isn't archived (`ConflictError`). The ruleset's `permissions` answer it for each role (`inviteRoles`), which its contributors' dialog offers as they are, its Invite shown while they hold any; `canInviteContributors`, `canManageAdminContributors` and `canUpdateContributorRoles` are answered for the public API alone, a contributor's row answering its own roles (`roles`) |
| `canRevokeContributor({ role, status })` | owner, **Admin** | archived or not; an Admin (`role`), the owner only; a contributor who's active or invited (`status`, `ConflictError`). A ruleset's contributors list answers it for each row (`canRemove`), and a character's its own (`CharactersPolicy.canRevokeContributor`) |
| `canUpdateContributorRole({ from, to, status })` | owner, **Admin** | to or from Admin, the owner only (who, first); on a ruleset that isn't archived (`ConflictError`); an active contributor's (`status`, `ConflictError`). Its contributors list answers, for each row, the roles it may take (`roles`): none, and its dialog offers no Edit Role |
| `canRead` | public rulesets: anyone; private: owner, any active contributor, a member (any role) of a campaign on it, archived or not, the owner or an active contributor of a character built on it | every request that names the ruleset by its id, a read or a write, asks it first. Throws `NotFoundError "Ruleset not found"`, the 404 an unknown ruleset gets, so a private ruleset's existence isn't revealed. `isReadable` answers the same yes or no |
| `canReadContributors` | owner, any contributor | lists the contributors |
| `canLeave` | any active contributor | leaving its contributors (`leaveRuleset`), archived or not. Anyone else, its owner too, contributes as no one and is answered as not found (`NotFoundError "You are not a contributor of this ruleset"`). Its `permissions` answer it (`canLeave`) |
| `canUnarchive` | **owner only** | Archived → Draft |
| `canViewChanges` | public rulesets: anyone; private: owner + any contributor | overrides metadata: a fork's alone, as a base inherits nothing (`UnprocessableEntityError`, on its kind). Its changes route, its restores, and its lists of its own entities alone (`childOnly`: `getEntities`, `getEntityGroups`) ask it; its page's Local Changes toggle and item follow it |
| `canRevertChange` | who edits its entities (owner, **Admin**, **Editor**) | reverting a fork's copy of an inherited entity to its parent's (`RulesetChangesService.revertOverride`): who first, then `canViewChanges` (a base has none, a 422), then its state (an archived one, a 409) |

A ruleset page offers what these answer (`permissions`, `GET /api/rulesets/:id`, which `useRulesetPermissions` reads): editing its entities and the ruleset, forking it, unarchiving it, seeing its contributors, inviting and managing them, the roles an invite may give (`inviteRoles`; a contributor's row answers its own, `roles`), publishing it, subscribing it to extensions and unsubscribing it from one (whether a character uses what would leave is answered when it's asked), seeing its local changes (`canViewChanges`), and the kinds its publish and its edit may give it (`kinds`). The client rebuilds none of them. It reads the owner itself in one place only, the menu's Help link, which isn't a right. Its archive dialog says the same to whoever archives it: only its owner can unarchive it.

Listing rulesets is enforced by the `findPage` scopes, not the policy. Every request that names a ruleset by its id goes through `canRead` before any other check: a read of it or of what's under it (its entities, owned entities, groups, templates, tables, progression, links, modifiers, requirements, target paths, Settings, extensions, changes, contributors, a new character's creation picks), and a write (its update, archive, unarchive, publish, fork and star, its contributors' invites, role changes, revokes and leave, its Settings, its entities', links', progressions', variants', customizations' and restores' saves, its extensions' subscribes and unsubscribes, and a character, an import or a campaign created on it). The services start with `getReadableRuleset` or `withReadableRuleset` (`server/services/rulesets/readableRuleset.ts`), which look the ruleset up, ask it, and hand on the policy, whose write checks (`canUpdate`, `canUpdateEntity`, `canCreateCharacter`…) then refuse a reader who may not write with their 403. Whoever may not read it gets the answer an unknown id gets, the same status and body, whatever else the request would have been refused for: a contributor's role change or revoke a contributor of a ruleset the session may not read is answered as an unknown contributor (`Contributor not found`), and an extension it may not read as an unknown one (`Extension not found`). An activity links to a ruleset, its content, its customizations and its contributors only when the session may read it (`findReadableRuleset`). `canViewChanges` and `canReadContributors` narrow reading further. A ruleset read as another's ancestor or extension, through that ruleset's view, isn't read by its id: its readers are that ruleset's. A character's reads (its sheet, its level-up, its inventory, its PDF) are gated by the character, not its ruleset. Rulesets are never hard-deleted: they're archived (`canUpdate`).

**What a refusal names.** A check on a change to a ruleset reads what the change reaches: the ruleset's view, and the rulesets built on it (`buildLineageCondition`: a fork of it, an extension's subscribers), which may be other users' private rulesets. What it finds in those it never names: the in-use check answers yes or no (`canDeleteEntity({ inUse })`), and a template's type change names an item its ruleset's view shows and counts those of the rulesets built on it (`ItemEntity.planEdit`). What no change of the ruleset reaches (another fork's items, made from the original or its own copy) isn't read at all.

**Listing scopes** (`RulesetsRepository.findPage`, used by the create-character wizard, dashboard, etc.):

| `scope` query param | Returns |
|---|---|
| `base` | System base rulesets: `system`, not a fork |
| `forked` | The user's own forks |
| `myDrafts` | Draft rulesets where the user is owner OR active contributor |
| `published` | Rulesets the user can play with: public ones must be `status = 'Published'`; owned and contributed-to ones bypass the status gate (Draft is fine — `Published` only governs outward discoverability for *public* rulesets). `kind = 'ruleset'`. Powers the character-creation and campaign-creation pickers — extensions are filtered out so they're never offered as a playable target |
| `community` | Public Published user forks with `kind = 'ruleset'` (`system = false`, `private = false`). Extensions (system or user) are filtered out — they live under the `extensions` scope |
| `campaignAccessible` | Rulesets the user has access to via campaign membership |
| `archived` | The user's archived rulesets (owner-only) |
| `extensions` | Published rulesets with `kind = 'extension'` — both system-seeded and user-published. Powers the subscribe-extension picker on a fork |
| `systems` | System rulesets (`system`) — bases plus published system extensions. Used for sitemap/SEO |
| `starred` | Anything the user starred |
| `contributedTo` | Rulesets where the user is an active contributor |
| `createdByMe` | Rulesets the user owns or actively contributes to |
| `createdByMePrivate` | The private rulesets the user owns |
| (no scope) | Default: everything the user can see (owned, system, campaign-accessible, contributed-to) |

**Where each ruleset type surfaces by scope:**

| Type / Scope | `published` | `community` | `extensions` | `systems` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base (system) | ✓ | – | – | ✓ |
| System extension (`kind='extension'`, `system`) | – | – | ✓ | ✓ |
| User fork as ruleset (`kind='ruleset'`) | ✓ | ✓ (if public) | – | – |
| User fork as extension (`kind='extension'`) | – | – | ✓ (if public) | – |

**A system ruleset is one `system` marks, not one without an owner.** Deleting an account leaves its rulesets without an owner (`Rulesets.orphan`), `system` still false. Such an orphaned ruleset is still a user's: the scopes list it by its privacy, a public one in `community` or `extensions`, a private one to its contributors and campaign members (and in `starred` to whoever starred it), and `RulesetsPolicy` reads it so: its contributors keep their role, so they read it, see its changes and its contributors, create characters, imports and campaigns on it, and leave it. It is frozen as its owner left it: with nobody left to manage its Admins, publish it or unarchive it, every change is refused to everyone, an Admin's and an Editor's too (`RulesetRoles.checkOwned`: its entities, the ruleset itself, its contributors, its publish, archive and unarchive, its extensions), with "… a ruleset without an owner", where a system ruleset's refusal says "… a base ruleset". An extension subscribed to must be public unless it's a system one, so an orphaned private extension is refused.

The character-creation wizard combines `published + campaignAccessible` (and historically `myDrafts`, now subsumed by `published` for owned/contributed drafts) to show the full eligible list. `myDrafts` is still useful as a focused "drafts I'm working on" filter.

## Characters — `CharactersPolicy`

`CharactersPolicy.for(tx, session, character)` loads, for a player character it doesn't own, whether the session is an active contributor (`contributorsInCharacter`, status `Active`) and whether it's a Game Master of the campaign the character is linked to (`PlayerCharacters.findCharacterIds(db, { campaignArchived: false, characterIds, gameMasterId })`: a live Game Master's seat in a campaign that isn't archived, the character linked from a live seat, whatever its visibility; `forMany` reads both for a page's player characters at once, the campaign roster's, a child's still as `for` does), and for a child (3.5's bonded creature) whether it edits its parent, a player character archived or not (the parent's own policy's `isEditor`), and whether that parent is archived: a child is its parent's editors', a player character its own editors' alone. A campaign's Game Master edits its characters as an active contributor does: their sheet and their own rows, not what's their owner's alone (their archive, portrait, share link, contributors or link to a campaign), and nothing of them once the campaign is archived, as an archived campaign is read-only. The schema has a `role` column (`Admin | Editor | Viewer`) for parity with ruleset contributors, but `CharacterContributorsService.inviteContributor` always creates rows as `Editor` and `CharactersPolicy` ignores the role — every active contributor has the same effective grant. The column is reserved for future tiering.

| Method | Allowed actors |
|---|---|
| `isReadable` | a player character's owner, its active contributors and its live campaign's Game Masters (its editors), a child's parent's, archived or not: the character's sheet (`CharactersService.getCharacter`), a revoke of one of its contributors and an activity's link to it, through `findReadableCharacter`. Anyone else gets the answer an unknown character gets |
| `canEdit` / `isEditable` | its readers, while neither it nor a child's parent is archived: its sheet's edit and its picks' picker, through `getEditableCharacter`, refused to anyone else as the 404 an unknown character gets and, archived, on its state (`ConflictError`), as an archived ruleset's or campaign's edit is; `isEditable` answers the same yes or no, which its PDF export asks (`canExport`) |
| `canExport` | its editors while they may edit it (`isEditable`); anyone else, an archived character's editors among them, gets the 404 an unknown character gets: its PDF export, which `CampaignsPolicy.canExportCharacter` asks too |
| `isEditor` | a player character's owner, its active contributors and the Game Masters of the live campaign it's linked to, archived or not (a child has none: its own rows are its parent's): its own rows' reads and writes (its levels, scores, inventory, modifiers), through `canReadRows` and `canEditRows`; in a campaign, who reads a Partial character whole and its private notes (`CampaignsPolicy`), and who gets its share token (`CampaignCharactersService`) |
| `canEditPortrait` / `isPortraitEditable` | its readers (anyone else gets the 404 an unknown character gets), of them a player character's **owner only** (a child's, any of its parent's editors) and never a demo's, each a `ForbiddenError` otherwise, while neither it nor a child's parent is archived (`ConflictError`): its portrait's upload, attach and removal, through `getPortraitEditableCharacter`, which is the portrait's `canAttach` (below). `isPortraitEditable` answers the same yes or no, as its `permissions`' `canEditPortrait` |
| `isPrivateNotesReader` | its readers (`isReadable`): its private notes, on its own sheet (`CharactersService.getCharacter`), in its PDF export (`findExportableCharacter`), and from a campaign with its Game Master (`CampaignsPolicy.isPrivateNotesReader`); each reading the engine takes (`show` or `hide`) from it (`toNotesReading`) |
| `canReadRows` | its editors (`isEditor`), archived or not: its own rows' reads (its inventory's and its modifiers' lists, and one entry or one modifier of them), through `getRowReadableCharacter`, and what `canEditRows` and `canReadContributors` start with. Anyone else, a child's parent's editors too, gets the 404 an unknown character gets |
| `canEditRows` / `isRowEditable` | `canReadRows`, while it isn't archived: its own rows (its levels, scores, inventory and modifiers), which its writes and the reads its edits make ask (`getRowEditableCharacter`), refused to anyone else as the 404 an unknown character gets and, archived, on its state (`ConflictError`); a child (3.5's bonded creature) has none, its own rows being its parent's. `isRowEditable` answers the same yes or no, as its `permissions`' `canEditRows`, which its page's levels, sheet rows, inventory (its editing section, else the sheet's own list) and Manage Modifiers follow |
| `canArchive` / `canUnarchive` | **owner only**, of a player character: archiving a live one, unarchiving an archived one (`ConflictError` on the other state, as a refusal on the record's state is). Anyone else, its contributors too, gets the 404 an unknown character gets. Its `permissions` answer both, as `canHardDelete` and `canExport`, so its page offers each action as the server decides it |
| `canHardDelete({ inActiveCampaign })` | its owner (`canOwn`), must be archived, must not be linked to an *active* campaign (links to archived campaigns don't block): the service looks the link up. Throws `ConflictError` on active-campaign link. See [persistence.md](./persistence.md#recoverable-user-content) |
| `canShare` | its owner (`canOwn`), while it isn't archived: creating and revoking its share token |
| `canLink(campaign, { link, seat })` / `isLinkable` | its owner (`canOwn`): linking it to a campaign from the session's seat (`CampaignCharactersService.linkCharacter`, within the campaign's own `canLinkCharacter`, after its seat and before its state), archived or not, as its lock reads it (`lockNamedCharacter`). The campaign takes one on its own ruleset (not a fork of it, nor its parent), that isn't archived, and linked to no other seat (`link`), each another record it can't take otherwise (`UnprocessableEntityError`); one its seat linked already is the campaign's own link (`ConflictError`: "already linked to this campaign"). `isLinkable` answers the same yes or no, which the link dialog's list asks of each of the session's characters (`getLinkableCharacters`) |
| `canReadContributors` | `canReadRows`, its editors: listing its contributors, and what leaving them starts with (`canLeave`). Anyone else gets the 404 an unknown character gets |
| `canLeave` | `canReadContributors`, then an active contributor: leaving its contributors (`leaveCharacter`), archived or not. Its owner contributes as no one and is answered as not found (`NotFoundError "You are not a contributor of this character"`). Its `permissions` answer it (`canLeave`) |
| `canManageContributors` | `canReadContributors`, then **owner only** (a contributor gets `ForbiddenError`): what inviting and revoking a contributor start with |
| `canRevokeContributor({ status })` | `canManageContributors`, of a contributor who's active or invited (`status`, `ConflictError`): revoking one. Its contributors list answers it for each row (`canRemove`), which its dialog's Remove follows |
| `canInviteContributors({ invitee })` | `canManageContributors`; a user other than its owner (`invitee`, the address's account: `UnprocessableEntityError`); on a character that isn't archived (`ConflictError`) |
| `canAcceptInvite` | anyone: accepting a contributor's invite (`{ status }`), a pending one (`canAnswerInvite`), on a character that isn't archived (`ConflictError`) |

Whoever may not read a character gets the answer an unknown id gets on its contributors, the same status and body: its list, an invite and a leave (`Character not found`), and a revoke of one of its contributors (`Contributor not found`, as an unknown contributor). An activity links to a character, its levels, its inventory and its contributors only when the session may read it (`findReadableCharacter`), archived or not; a character contributor's, its invitee to the invite while it's pending, and to no access once it's revoked or rejected. `CharactersService.getCharacter` answers what its reader may do with it, as `permissions`, each from the policy (`describeCharacterPermissions`, `server/services/characters/characterPermissions.ts`: a check through `isAllowed`, `server/services/policies/isAllowed.ts`, as `RulesetsService.getRuleset` does), and so does a campaign's character (below), its export the campaign's: `canArchive`, `canUnarchive`, `canHardDelete` (with no active campaign: a link to one is its delete's answer), `canEdit` (`isEditable`), `canEditPortrait` (`isPortraitEditable`), `canEditRows` (`isRowEditable`), `canExport`, `canInviteContributors`, `canLeave` (an active contributor), `canManageContributors`, `canReadContributors` and `canShare`. A shared sheet answers each of them false, to whoever reads it, its owner too (`CharacterSharingService.getSharedCharacter`, `SHARE_LINK_PERMISSIONS`): its link gives its reader no role. The client's `useCharacterPermissions` reads them, and its page offers each action as they answer it, the character's state with it: the client decides none of them, a demo's refusals among them, which are the policy's too.

**A player character's own rows are edited through `getRowEditableCharacter`.** `getRowEditableCharacter(db, session, characterId)` (`server/services/characters/editableCharacter.ts`) looks the character up, archived or not, and returns it once its policy's `canEditRows` lets the session edit its rows (owner OR active contributor OR live campaign's Game Master of a player character, a 404 for anyone else; an archived one refused on its state, a 409), and the services that write its levels, scores, inventory and modifiers call it as their first guard, or, for an item's or a modifier's removal, ask `canEditRows` of the policy their write's lock hands them (`withLockedCharacter`); their transactions read the character archived or not (`withCharacterWrite`, `withLockedCharacter`), so its policy decides, and the rest of the function can assume edit rights; a child's own rows are refused, its levels being its parent's. `getRowReadableCharacter` (same module) is its rows' read, an archived one's too, its inventory's and its modifiers' (`canReadRows`). `getEditableCharacter(db, session, characterId)` (same module) asks its policy's `canEdit`, a child through its parent, and `getPortraitEditableCharacter` its `canEditPortrait`: each guard is named for the check it asks, and looks the character up archived or not, an unknown one the 404 its check answers whoever may not read it. `findReadableCharacter(db, session, characterId)` (same module) looks the character up, archived or not, and hands on it and its policy iff its policy's `isReadable` holds, or nothing, as for an unknown character. Who edits a character is decided there alone: the repositories' `editorId` filter is a list's (`Characters.findRulesetIds(db, { editorId, rulesetIds })`, which `RulesetsPolicy` asks), never one character's. Reading a character in a campaign is gated by the link's visibility (`link.visibility`); creating one, by the ruleset access check below.

**Character creation against a ruleset** — `CharactersService.createCharacter` (and a character's import) calls `RulesetsPolicy.canCreateCharacter` through `withCharacterCreate`, and `CampaignsService.createCampaign` calls `canCreateCampaign` through `withCampaignCreate`, which applies the same rule: each write's helper (`server/services/writes/`) asks `getReadableRuleset` and hands on its policy. A deleted ruleset, or one the session may not read (`canRead`), is a 404 (`Ruleset not found`) before the policy runs; then who may (below; `ForbiddenError` otherwise), and then a ruleset a character can be on: an extension or an archived ruleset is refused (`UnprocessableEntityError "Choose an active playable ruleset"`), another record the request names, after who. Who may is one of:

1. Public, past its draft (`!ruleset.private && status !== "Draft"`: published, or archived since, whose refusal is then its state's, the 422 above), OR
2. Owned by the session user, OR
3. The session user is an active contributor on it, OR
4. The session user is a player, from a live seat, on a live campaign that uses this ruleset (a member of an archived campaign reads the ruleset, but doesn't create on it).

If none match → `ForbiddenError "You do not have access to this ruleset"`.

Campaign character responses leave the private notes out of the identity's block for viewers without character edit rights or GM status (a bonded creature shows in its master's sheet as a subsheet, which holds no notes; its own sheet goes to its editors alone); the client shows the field when the answer holds it, read-only there. The character's own sheet (`GET /characters/:id`) goes to its editors alone, private notes included (its policy's `isPrivateNotesReader`), and a share link's response leaves them out. Share tokens are returned only to character editors: a share token is the server's access decision alone. The engine's sheet answers none (its `shareToken` `null`, `CharacterColumns`), and the service that knows the session's rights writes it: the character's own sheet (`CharactersService.getCharacter`, its editors') its row's, a campaign character's (`CampaignCharactersService.getCharacter`) its row's for an editor and `null` for anyone else, and a share link's the token its reader sent, which they hold already. A Partial response (the engine's `character(input).describeForMember`, its `partial` reading) is an explicit allowlist, which the core's characters part builds from its shapes' declared keys whatever the ruleset answers (`PartialSheets`, `PartialSheet`): the character's row's columns and ruleset, `shareToken` `null`, empty `requirements` and `modifiers`, a valid `validation`, a summary with no tracks, and its identity's `fields` block alone, its head and its fields without its picked links or notes. Which fields that block holds is its ruleset's (`describePartial`: 3.5's race and physical traits, age, gender, height, weight): no description, languages, beliefs, notes, level or build. A printed sheet reads as its route does (`describeSheet`'s reading): a share link's PDF without the private notes, the PDF export's as its policy says who reads them, which `findExportableCharacter` answers (`CharactersPolicy.isPrivateNotesReader`, or from a campaign `CampaignsPolicy.isPrivateNotesReader`: its exporters, each a reader of them); neither is built from the share token.

## Campaigns — `CampaignsPolicy`

`CampaignsPolicy.for(tx, session, campaign)` loads the session's live player row (`playersInCampaign`), in a campaign archived or not: archiving a campaign leaves its seats alone, and a seat is archived only with its user's account, which no session signs in to (0096 restored the seats a campaign archived before then had archived with it). The PDF worker, which has no session, builds one for the export's user and asks `isGameMaster()`.

A campaign keeps a Game Master: `CampaignPlayersService` neither removes nor demotes its last one, and the campaign's `permissions` answer that they may not leave it (`isLastGameMaster`, `isLastGameMasterOf`: `server/services/campaigns/lastGameMaster.ts`, which the players list and the campaign's read ask as the writes do). An account's deletion makes sure the campaign keeps a Game Master instead, archived campaign or not: its longest-seated other player becomes Game Master, or, with nobody else seated, the campaign is archived (`AccountService.handOnCampaigns`, [persistence.md](./persistence.md#campaign-membership-rows-players-invites-playercharacters)).

| Method | Allowed actors |
|---|---|
| `canRead` | any member, Player or Game Master, of the campaign archived or not: returns their player row. Every request that names the campaign by its id, a read or a write, asks it first (below). Anyone else, a pending invitee, a removed player or a seat archived with its account among them, gets `NotFoundError "Campaign not found"`, the 404 an unknown campaign gets, so a campaign's existence isn't revealed. `isReadable` answers the same yes or no |
| `canSeeCharacter` | a member, on a character linked to the campaign; a **Private** one its linking player and the Game Master alone (`isGameMaster`), refused to the others as a 404, as a character not linked is: the campaign character's page (`CampaignCharactersService.getCharacter`) and an activity's link to it (`findVisibleCampaignCharacter`, which answers none) |
| `canSeeCharacters` / `isPartialReader` / `isPrivateNotesReader` | the campaign's characters as a member reads them: its roster's links (every one for the Game Master; a player's, all but other seats' Private ones), a **Partial** character read whole by its player, the Game Master and its editors and partially by the others (`isPartialReader(link, characterPolicy)`), and its private notes read by those who read them of the character itself (`CharactersPolicy.isPrivateNotesReader`) and the Game Master alone (`CampaignCharactersService`, and its PDF export from the campaign, `findExportableCharacter`) |
| `canUpdate` | **Game Master only** (`isGameMaster`) |
| `isAddressReader` / `isSlotAddressReader` | the campaign's email addresses, its player slots' and its invites': **Game Master only** reads them and searches by them (`isAddressReader`); in the players list, a player reads their own slot's (`isSlotAddressReader(userId)`), the others' left out (`campaignAddresses.ts`); the invites list leaves every address out for a player |
| `canManagePlayers` | **Game Master only**, on a campaign that isn't archived (`canModify` and `canUpdate`): adding a player, editing one, and removing another's seat (`CampaignPlayersService`) |
| `canRemovePlayer(player, { isLastGameMaster })` / `canUpdatePlayer(player, { isLastGameMaster, role })` | removing a seat: its own player's leave (`canModify`), another's the Game Master's (`canManagePlayers`); changing one, the Game Master's. A seat archived with its user's account is not found; the last Game Master is never removed nor demoted (`ConflictError`). The players list answers each row's `canEdit`, `canRemove` and `canRevokeInvite` (`canRevokeInvite` of its pending invite), which its rows offer; the campaign's `permissions` answer `canRemovePlayer` of the session's own seat (`canLeave`) |
| `canInviteToSeat(player, { pendingInvite })` | `canManagePlayers`: inviting someone to a seat no user holds (`ConflictError`), without a pending invite (`ConflictError`), the seat's state. A seat archived with its user's account is not found first, as `canUpdatePlayer` and `canRemovePlayer` refuse it. Whether the invitee already plays in the campaign, or is invited to it, is the invite's own check (`CampaignPlayersService`), a duplicate row's 409, as a contributor's is (`checkNewContributor`). The players list answers it for each row (`canInvite`), which the Edit Player dialog's address field and its prompt to send an invite follow |
| `canArchive` / `canUnarchive` | **Game Master only**: archiving a campaign that isn't archived (`canModify`), unarchiving one that is (`ConflictError`, as a refusal on the record's state is) |
| `canEdit` | **Game Master only** (`canUpdate`), on a campaign that isn't archived (`canModify`): its name and description |
| `canAcceptInvite` | anyone without a seat in it: accepting an invite to a seat (`{ status }`), a pending one (`canAnswerInvite`), in a campaign that isn't archived (`canModify`; `ConflictError` for an archived one, and then for who holds a seat already, an invite they took over with an address while seated under another). Under the campaign's lock, so two acceptances take turns |
| `canHardDelete` | **Game Master only**, must be archived. See [persistence.md](./persistence.md#recoverable-user-content) |
| `canModify` | anyone the other checks allow, on a campaign that isn't archived (archived campaigns are read-only; a Game Master can still revoke an invite) |
| `canExportCharacter` | a character linked to the campaign's PDF export from it: the **Game Master**, or whoever exports the character themselves (its `CharactersPolicy.canExport`, its editors'), archived campaign or not: `findExportableCharacter`, and the campaign character's `permissions.canExport` |
| `canLinkCharacter(character, link)` | any member, Player or Game Master, of a campaign that isn't archived (a seat, `canRead`, and `canModify`), the character a link names (`character`, its policy, and its `link`, another record: its `canLink`, from the seat) checked between the two: linking a character (`CampaignCharactersService.linkCharacter`), setting the visibility of one the session linked (`updateCharacterVisibility`, through `canSetCharacterVisibility`, which asks the same seat and state, and between them matches the link to the seat), and listing those the session could link, so the link dialog lists only what linking accepts (`GET /characters/unlinked/:campaignId`, `getLinkableCharacters`: of the session's own player characters, archived or not, those each one's `isLinkable` takes from the seat, read whole and then paged). The campaign's `canLinkCharacters` answers it, which shows Link Character; each roster row's `canSetVisibility` answers `canSetCharacterVisibility` on its link, which shows its visibility select |

Every request that names a campaign by its id goes through `canRead`, and whoever may not read the campaign gets the answer an unknown id gets before anything else would refuse them: a read of it or of what's under it (the campaign, its players, invites and characters, a character of it, the characters the session could link to it), and a write (its update, archive, unarchive and permanent delete, its players' slots added, changed and removed, a character linked, a linked character's visibility, a linked character's PDF). The services start with `getReadableCampaign` (`server/services/campaigns/readableCampaign.ts`), which looks the campaign up (archived or not: its update, archive, unarchive and permanent delete are refused on the wrong state by their policy, `canEdit`, `canArchive`, `canUnarchive`, `canHardDelete`, which its `permissions` answer the page with), asks it, and hands on the policy and the session's seat, whose write checks (`canUpdate`, `canDelete`, `canModify`…) then refuse a member who may not write with their 403. Whoever may not read it gets the answer an unknown id gets, the same status and body, whatever else the request would have been refused for. An invite's revoke (`revokeInvite`), which names the invite, looks up its slot, archived or not, and its campaign (`findReadableCampaign`) first, and answers whoever may not read the campaign as an unknown invite (`Invite not found`). A linked character's PDF looks the character's link to the campaign up first (`findExportableCharacter`), then asks `isReadable`: whoever may not read the campaign, its editor too, gets what the export of an unknown campaign gets (`Character not found in this campaign`), either way. An activity links to a campaign, its players and invites only when the session may read it (`findReadableCampaign`), and to a campaign's character only when the session sees it there (`findVisibleCampaignCharacter`, which asks `canSeeCharacter`); a campaign invite's, its invitee to the invite (`/campaign-invite/:inviteId`, whose page says what became of an answered or revoked one) until they've accepted it. The invite flow reads by the invite, never the campaign's id: its invitee reads it (`GET /campaigns/invites/:inviteId`, the campaign's name and state with it), accepts or rejects it, and reads the campaign once they've accepted it.

**Campaign character visibility** is *not* a CAS-protected surface — only the linking player can change visibility on their own character (`CampaignsPolicy.canSetCharacterVisibility` throws `ForbiddenError "You do not own this character in this campaign"` for every other member who sees the link, and the 404 a character not linked to the campaign gets for one they don't see, another seat's Private one, including the GM: an identity match on the link's player; a non-member gets the 404 an unknown campaign gets first, `canRead`). This is single-user contention by design.

**Partial visibility filtering**: who sees a Partial character trimmed is decided one way, for the roster and the character page alike (`CampaignsPolicy.isPartialReader`): anyone but the link's player, the Game Master and the character's editors (`CharactersPolicy.isEditor`: its owner, an active contributor, or its live campaign's Game Master; the roster reads the session's contributions to its page's characters, and its Game Master's seats in their campaigns, in one query each). `getCharacters` sends such a character with its name and its race's chip only: its card is the engine's for a partial reader (`CharactersPart.describeCard`, through `describeCharacterCards`, which the character lists' cards are too), its `description` `null` and its chips those its ruleset marks as its partial reading's; every card, whole or partial, is described from its row without its private notes or share token, each chip its declared keys alone. The service adds the link's facts alone (`visibility`, `isOwn`: the policy's `isLinkOwner`, whether the link is on the session's seat, which holds the links its user made and those handed on to it), what the session may do to it (`canSetVisibility`) and `isPartial: true`, which its card reads to say its identity is private (`PARTIAL_IDENTITY_NOTE`, the sheet's words) rather than that it has no description.

**What the campaign-character detail response answers**: `CampaignCharactersService.getCharacter` returns `isOwner` (link-slot ownership in the campaign) and `permissions`, what the session may do with the character as its own sheet answers them (`describeCharacterPermissions`: its `canEdit`, `CharactersPolicy.isEditable`, is its owner's, its active contributors' and, while the campaign isn't archived, its Game Masters' on a linked character, which is a live player character), but `canExport`, the campaign's (`CampaignsPolicy.canExportCharacter`: the Game Master, or the character's `canExport`): the public API's campaign character sheet answers them. The campaign character view shows "Edit Character" to `permissions.canEdit` only, and "Download PDF" to `permissions.canExport` — `isOwner` is kept on the response for any future UI that needs the strict campaign-link semantics.

**PDF export of a campaign character** (`POST /campaigns/:id/characters/:characterId/pdf`): the character must be linked to the campaign, which the session may read (a member); its editors and the Game Master may export it (`CampaignsPolicy.canExportCharacter`, over the character's `canExport`), whatever its visibility, since the GM already sees the full sheet. Archived campaigns still allow it: archiving makes a campaign read-only, and its character pages stay viewable. Every refusal is a 404, as for the character's own `POST /characters/:id/pdf`. Both go through `findExportableCharacter`, which the PDF worker runs again when the job starts, so access lost in between cancels the export.

## Attachments — `AttachmentsService` registry

Attachments (avatars, character portraits) have no policy of their own: the `ATTACHABLE_TYPES` map in `AttachmentsService` registers, for each record type, its record's policy's guard (`canAttach`), which refuses a session that may not write its attachments, asked before a write's work and again under its record's lock (`withAttachmentWrite`, which locks a character archived or not), as the writes before it left the record. Each throws its policy's refusal, which the service awaits: an avatar's `UsersPolicy.canEditAvatar` (`getAvatarEditableUser`), a `ForbiddenError "Not authorized to attach to this record"`; a portrait's `CharactersPolicy.canEditPortrait` (`getPortraitEditableCharacter`).

| Record type | Write (`canAttach`) | Read |
|---|---|---|
| `User` (avatar) | its policy's `canEditAvatar` (`getAvatarEditableUser` is its `canAttach`): the user's own session, not a demo's; anyone else, an unknown user's id too, a `ForbiddenError` (403) | any authenticated session |
| `Character` (portrait) | its policy's `canEditPortrait` (`getPortraitEditableCharacter` is its `canAttach`): a player character's **owner only**; a bonded character's: whoever can edit its master (owner, contributor or its campaign's Game Master). Anyone who may not read it gets the 404 an unknown character gets, a reader who may not change it (a contributor, its campaign's Game Master, a demo) a 403, and an archived one, or a child of one, its 409 | any authenticated session |

Note the divergence: a character's active contributor, and its live campaign's Game Master, **can edit the character** (`getEditableCharacter`) but **cannot upload or replace its portrait** — a player character's portrait writes are owner-only. If that's not the desired behavior, change `CharactersPolicy.canEditPortrait` to also accept its other editors: the character page offers what it answers.

Reads are intentionally permissive, with no check of their own, since avatar/portrait URLs add no exposure beyond pages that are already gated: a record type that needs one adds its read checker to `ATTACHABLE_TYPES`.

## Invites (rulesets / campaigns / character contributors)

The three invite lifecycles share the same shape:

| Action | Gate |
|---|---|
| Send / re-send / revoke an invite | The policy that gates managing the parent: `RulesetsPolicy.canInviteContributors({ invitee, role })` and `canRevokeContributor({ role, status })`, `CampaignsPolicy.canInviteToSeat(player, { pendingInvite })` to send (a seat archived with its user's account not found first; `canManagePlayers`: GM, a campaign that isn't archived; then a seat no user holds, without a pending invite, `ConflictError`) and `canRevokeInvite({ status })` to revoke (GM, an archived campaign's too; only a pending invite, `ConflictError` otherwise), or `CharactersPolicy.canInviteContributors` and `canRevokeContributor({ status })` (owner) |
| Accept / reject | Identity match: `invite.userId !== session.userId` in `getOwnInvite` (`server/services/invites/inviteAnswers.ts`), which every invite service reads the invite through, a campaign's and a contributor's row alike. Throws `NotFoundError` rather than `ForbiddenError` to avoid leaking the existence of invites addressed to other users. Its record's policy then decides the rest, whatever the invite's kind: a reject asks `canAnswerInvite({ status })` (every policy's, `BasePolicy`: a pending invite, a `ConflictError` for an answered or revoked one), an accept `canAcceptInvite({ status })`, which asks it first, then refuses an archived ruleset, character or campaign, and who already holds a seat in the campaign (`ConflictError`). The invitee's read of the invite answers both (`canAccept`, `canReject`) and why an accept is refused, in its refusal's words (`acceptRefusal`, `readRefusal`), one way for every kind (`describeInviteAnswers`), so the invite's page offers Accept and Reject as they would be taken, a refused accept's Reject too, and says what it says rather than guessing, a seated invitee's campaign beside it (`isSeated`). Its notifications carry the same answers (`inviteAnswers`), which the bell, the dashboard and the notifications page offer, never whether the notification is read, and Mark All as Read leaves an invite's notification unread while its invite may still be answered. Both read their records' policies one way, `server/services/invites/invitedRecords.ts`: an invite's page its invite's (`readCampaignInvitePolicies`, `readCharacterInvitePolicies`, `readRulesetInvitePolicies`), a page of notifications its invites' and their answers at once (`readCampaignInviteAnswers`…) |
| Leave (self-remove from contributor or self-remove from campaign) | Its record's policy: `RulesetsPolicy.canLeave` and `CharactersPolicy.canLeave` (an active contributor; anyone else, its owner too, a `NotFoundError`), or `CampaignsPolicy.canRemovePlayer` on the session's own seat (a campaign that isn't archived, `canModify`; never its last Game Master, `ConflictError`) |

The invite services' `acceptInvite` and `rejectInvite` (campaigns, rulesets and characters) follow this pattern, through `getOwnInvite`: an identity match, not a permission gate — see "Identity vs. policy" below.

## Customizations

Modifiers, fields, and requirements live on a parent entity (a feat, item, class, race, spell, class level, modifier, or character), fields on a ruleset too. They have no policy of their own: the gating is whichever policy owns the parent's, and the engine's customization operations check that the parent exists in the ruleset's view (`CustomizedEntity`, `engine/core/customizations/`):

- Customizations on a **ruleset entity** are gated by `RulesetsPolicy.canUpdateEntity` (owner / Admin / Editor). Its fields are written by its own save, gated as its save is; a ruleset's own, its Settings (`RulesetSettingsService.updateSettings`), by `canUpdateEntity` too: who edits its content edits them, never a system ruleset.
- Customizations on a **character** (e.g. character modifiers) go through `CharactersService` and are gated by `canEditRows` (`getRowEditableCharacter`: owner / contributor / its live campaign's Game Master).

A ruleset entity's customization plans (`Engine.for(scope).modifiers(entityType, entityId).planCreate`, `.requirements(…).planDelete`…) refuse as not found (a 404) a parent the composed ruleset doesn't have (`CustomizedEntity.find`), and answer its display name for activity logging; before an update or a delete, `CustomizationEdit.cowCustomization` reads, in the write's transaction, the parent the row is on and the row itself, and refuses as not found (a 404) one no longer stored.

## Identity vs. policy

There are still a few inline `entity.userId === session.userId` checks in services. Those are **identity matches**, not permission gates — they're answering "is this me?" rather than "may I do this?". Keep them inline, don't move them into a policy:

- `getOwnInvite`, which the invite services' `acceptInvite` and `rejectInvite` read the invite through: "is this invite addressed to me?"
- OAuth-link checks: "did I just link this account to myself?"
- Branching predicates: an activity's link for the session's own invite or contribution (`ActivitiesService`'s `contributorUrl` and `inviteUrl`: `contributor.userId === session.userId`, `invite.userId === session.userId`) picks where the link goes, doesn't gate it.

Anything that *throws* on the basis of ownership is a permission gate and belongs in a policy. The "Permission Checks (Policy vs. Identity)" section of [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) is the canonical guidance.
